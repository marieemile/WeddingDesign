Six mock-ups drawn from the references in Stationary/. Everything here is a plain rectangle on a standard pre-cut size, so there is nothing to trim and no die to pay for. Every piece carries the same painted wavy border — the one from your third reference — and every number is a figure, not a word, so the cards read identically to your side and to André's. Colour and line are laid down as watercolour: three pigment passes, ragged edges, mottled density, paper tooth underneath.

Table numbersA6 · twelve tables

One border, three amounts of decoration

All three are A6 — 105 × 148 mm — which is a size you can buy as blank card by the hundred and feed through a printer. It also drops straight into an A6 standing frame if you want the brass look from your second reference. The routes differ only in what sits inside the border, so you can mix them: plain for the far tables, posies for the top table.

One to twelve. Worth seeing the whole run before you commit: Cormorant's 1 is a narrow figure with a full base serif, so 1, 11 and 12 sit lighter than the rest. If that bothers you on the day, the swap is Jost for the figures only — everything else stays.

The one alternative worth a look. Cormorant above, Jost below, same border. Jost's figures are geometric and unmistakable — 1 is a bare stem, 10 can't be misread — but they're cooler and more modern against a painted border. Cormorant is the brand's display face and the warmer of the two; it's the default here. Your call, and it only affects the figures.

A5 — 148 × 210 mm — again pre-cut, again nothing to trim. Dishes below are placeholder copy set at realistic lengths, so when the tasting is done you can drop the real ones in and nothing about the layout moves.

How the watercolour is made

Why it looks wet

Three passes, not one

Every painted mark is laid down three times: a wide bleed halo at low opacity, the body of the stroke, then a narrow darker core in Moss drawn back over the top. That gradient from pale edge to loaded centre is what the eye reads as pigment.

The edge is eaten

Each pass is pushed around by its own coarse noise, so no two passes share an outline. Nothing in these pieces has a clean vector edge — including the figures.

Density is mottled

A second, finer noise punches holes in each pass, so pigment pools and thins along the stroke the way it does when a loaded brush runs dry. This is the step that separates watercolour from a soft-focus filter.

Paper tooth underneath

A faint cold-press grain sits under the artwork on every sheet. It is what stops the ivory reading as screen-white.

Rule check

Against the brand book

Green is the stage, colour is flowers

Sage and Moss carry the border on all six pieces. The only other colour is Blush, and it only ever arrives as a flower — never as a flat field.

Three doodles is plenty

Table number 2 and Menu B run three each. Your fourth reference stacks eleven along the foot and reads as clipart; the same doodles, three of them, read as drawing.

Doodles, painted

These are your own 88 SVGs, re-laid in pigment instead of pen so they sit in the same world as the border. That's the one deliberate departure from the monoline rule — pen line next to painted border would have fought.

The hand, four words at most

WindSong carries Menu and Table no. and nothing else. Dishes are Cormorant italic; course names are Jost letterspaced caps.

No wordmark at table height

The M & A wordmark appears on none of these. It belongs on the invitation, the envelope seal and the order of service — beside a painted border the copperplate would lose.

Antique Rose stays home

Not used here. It signs the wordmark and nothing else; a large soft pink is Blush's job, and that's what the posies are.

To print

The order, in one table

Table numbers12 + 3 spares at A6, 105 × 148 mm. Buy blank A6 card, 300–350 gsm, cotton or a textured ivory. No trimming, no die, no bleed.
Menus120 at A5, 148 × 210 mm. Same stock, same story — pre-cut, straight trim.
At home vs. printerA home inkjet on 300 gsm will do these; the risk is the border, since a wide painted stroke shows banding on cheap paper. Print one of each before you commit to 132 sheets. A digital press will handle the pigment gradients without argument.
ColourSage #A8C29E with Moss #889063 at the pigment edge; Blush #F4B6C8 for flowers; Evening Fern #4A5A3D for all text. Give the printer the hexes, never "the green from the website".
TypeCormorant Garamond, Jost and WindSong — all free under the Open Font Licence, print use included, and all three carry André, sobremesa, pastéis and começar. Send the fonts with the artwork.
ArtworkDoodles come from Brand/doodles/ as vectors. Don't let anyone swap them for stock line icons — the wobble is the point.
If you change your mind on cuttingOne straight cut across A5 gives you a 105 × 148 menu that matches the table numbers exactly. Two cuts out of A4 gives you the 99 × 210 shape your references actually are. Both are still die-free.

My pick: table-number Route 1 with menu Route B. The numbers are the one piece on the table that has a job to do — finding a seat — and stripping them to a border and a figure does that job best, while the tablescape lands on the menu, which people actually pick up and read. If you'd rather the pink appeared on the table at all, take Route 3 and Menu C together; they're built to pair, and they're the closest to the framed reference you saved.
