01The anchor

Three words, locked

Every decision in this kit answers to these three. Say them to every supplier before you say anything else.

One

Wild

The garden left a little unstyled, and the Portuguese hillside in June. It's the word that tells a florist to stop arranging.

Two

Romantic

The script logo, watercolour, ribbon and a wavy edge on everything. Every piece of the suite is written in this register.

Three

Joyful

The word for how guests feel, and the one that keeps romantic from turning solemn. It's why butter yellow works hardest of the accents.

Green and ivory are the stage. The flowers are the colour. And every printed thing looks drawn by hand, never designed on a screen.

04The wordmark

M & A, as a true vector

Traced from your own logo file: five closed contours, no font involved. It holds from a 25 mm wax seal to a two-metre welcome sign. Canonical rose is #DE9687, the artwork's own ink. Always give printers the hex.

32 mm

Minimum printed width. In wax the hairline joins close up, so proof in real wax before cutting a die.

Fern

The default colourway on ivory. Rose is the romantic alternative.

Clear

Never let a doodle touch the wordmark. Formal copperplate and a loose pen line clash up close.

Never

Butter on ivory, or sage on ivory below display size. Never retype the logo in a font.

06Doodles

Eighty-eight, drawn for this wedding

The two of you, the venue, the food and glassware you'll actually serve, and every bloom in the palette. Monoline, outline only, one ink per piece. Tap any doodle to download it in the colour you've picked.

Ink
1.9

One line weight on a 100-unit grid, about 1.3 pt at 25 mm. Round caps, no thick-thin.

One ink

Per piece: a menu all in fern, a bar sign all in coral. Mixing inks on one sheet makes it look like clipart.

Three

Doodles per piece is plenty; six is too many. Never put them on the same piece as the house watercolour.

35 mm

Minimum size for the six couple figures. They're traced from your art, so they're filled shapes, not strokes.

07Painted pieces

The illustrations from your menu and drinks cards, each cut out on a transparent background, plus the painted wavy border. This is the register for the table suite: pigment, not pen.

09Tees

Wedding red, on cotton

Every tee is printed in Wedding Red #CB484D, sampled from your inspiration shirt. That makes it the one exception to “peony is the red”. All type is converted to outlines, so no font travels to the printer.

10The rules

Hand this to every vendor

Always

  • ✓Lead with Wild · Romantic · Joyful. Never say “organic”, which suppliers hear as beige.
  • ✓Ivory paper, Evening Fern ink. No black anywhere.
  • ✓Let colour arrive as flowers, with at most two flat colour areas per piece.
  • ✓Round shapes: ovals, scallops, wavy painted borders, bows and circles.
  • ✓Antique Brass for anything metal: foil, wax rims, cutlery, candlesticks.
  • ✓One doodle ink per piece, and three doodles at most.
  • ✓Digits on table numbers, set in Jost 300 so the 1 reads as a 1.
  • ✓Shape WindSong with ligatures on. Its letters join contextually.

Never

  • ✕A neutral or cream scheme with pastel accents. The colour is meant to be loud.
  • ✕Butter yellow as a line or text on ivory. It disappears.
  • ✕Antique Rose on anything larger than a ribbon.
  • ✕Silver, rose gold or chrome.
  • ✕A doodle touching the wordmark, or on the same piece as the venue watercolour.
  • ✕A casual marker script. The hand is WindSong, and it's delicate.
  • ✕Powder blue printed. It's for flowers only; lines use Swallow Blue.
  • ✕Die-cut or trimmed stationery. Use rectangular card only.
